How to Protect Yourself from Phishing

Cyber Security Awareness Module

What is Phishing?

Phishing is a type of cyber attack where criminals impersonate a trustworthy organization or person (such as a bank, a company, or even a friend) in order to trick victims into revealing sensitive information. This can include passwords, credit card numbers, or personal identification details.

Attackers usually send fake emails, text messages, or create fake websites that look almost identical to the real ones. Once a victim enters their information or clicks a malicious link, the attacker can steal their data, money, or identity.

How to Recognize Phishing

  • Suspicious sender address: The email address looks similar to a real one but has small differences (e.g., "support@paypa1.com" instead of "support@paypal.com").
  • Urgent or threatening language: Messages that pressure you to act immediately ("Your account will be suspended in 24 hours!").
  • Generic greetings: Emails that say "Dear Customer" instead of using your actual name.
  • Suspicious links: Hovering over a link shows a URL that doesn't match the company's real website.
  • Spelling and grammar mistakes: Legitimate companies rarely send emails with obvious errors.
  • Unexpected attachments: Files you weren't expecting, especially .exe, .zip, or .scr files.

Real-World Examples

Below are simulated examples of what phishing attempts typically look like:

⚠ Simulated Example

From: security@paypa1-support.com

Subject: Urgent: Your account has been limited!

Dear Customer,

We noticed unusual activity on your account. Your access has been limited. Please verify your information within 24 hours to avoid permanent suspension.

🚩 Red flags: misspelled domain, urgent threat, generic greeting, suspicious button

⚠ Simulated Example

From: delivery@dhl-tracking-update.com

Subject: Your package could not be delivered

Your parcel is waiting. A redelivery fee of $1.99 is required to release your package.

🚩 Red flags: unexpected fee request, fake shipping domain, urgency

Social Engineering Tactics

Social engineering is the psychological manipulation of people into performing actions or revealing confidential information. Attackers exploit human emotions rather than technical vulnerabilities.

Common tactics include:

  • Urgency: Creating a sense of panic so the victim acts without thinking.
  • Authority: Pretending to be a boss, IT department, or government official.
  • Fear: Threatening consequences like account suspension or legal action.
  • Curiosity: Using enticing subject lines like "You won a prize!" to get clicks.
  • Trust: Impersonating someone the victim knows, such as a colleague or friend.

How to Protect Yourself

  • Always check the sender's email address carefully before clicking any link.
  • Never enter your password or personal information through a link in an email — go directly to the official website instead.
  • Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) on all important accounts.
  • Hover over links to preview the actual URL before clicking.
  • Keep your software, browser, and antivirus up to date.
  • If a message seems suspicious, verify it directly with the company or person through a separate, trusted channel.
  • Report phishing emails to your IT department or email provider instead of just deleting them.
